## 1. What this project studies

The project asks whether a small language model can recognize, judge, and
repair its own mistakes without being given the correct answer.

Correctness is determined by objective tools:

- SymPy and deterministic answer parsing for mathematics.
- Executed tests for code.
- An LLM is never the correctness oracle.

The central distinction throughout the project is:

- **Detection:** recognizing that an answer may be wrong.
- **Selection:** choosing the correct answer among alternatives.
- **Repair:** producing a correct replacement.
- **Harness performance:** gains produced by external sampling, voting,
  probes, routing, or verification.
- **Autonomous self-correction:** the standalone model detects and repairs its
  own errors without external correctness information.

Do not merge these claims. A hidden-state probe is an external diagnostic.
Status feedback is assisted repair. Voting is a sampling harness. None alone
proves autonomous self-correction.

## 3. Current headline result

The project found that a 7B model contains a real but imperfect ability to
distinguish which of two conflicting solutions is correct. When judged in only
one presentation order, performance often looks close to guessing. Across
both orders, a positive skill signal appears consistently.

The main limitation is not primarily position bias. The judge often fails to
make a valid A/B choice: it emits format errors, loops until the token cap,
re-solves the task, or invents a third answer. Forcing a final
`Verdict: Solution A/B` line removes much of this failure.

At the system level, self-arbitration does not beat simple voting:

- A judge loses to vote@3 in 15 of 16 model-dataset cells.
- Self-check is approximately tied with vote@3.
- Vote@5 beats essentially every two-candidate strategy.
- Probe-weighted vote@3 improves over vote@3 out of sample for Llama and
  Mistral, but not for Self_Correction or Qwen, and still does not beat vote@5.

Overall, every robust accuracy gain comes from a harness around an unchanged
model: blind re-solving, routing, or voting. Model-level judgment skill exists,
but training mostly changes how that skill is expressed rather than creating a
reliably superior autonomous correction policy.

## 5. Phase history

### Phase 1 — verified critique and correction

Produced `Kxck/Self_Correction_v1`. Guided correction worked, especially for
code, but autonomous mathematics correction was weak. SFT largely taught the
correction format. It did not establish reliable autonomous detection.

### Phase 2 — KTO exploration

The verifier-labelled preference-data path was validated. No completed KTO
adapter was produced. This is historical infrastructure, not an active model.

### Phase 3 — KEEP/REVISE router

Closed 2026-09-13.

- Decision-Only V1: 65.0% balanced accuracy, 81% KEEP recall, 49% REVISE
  recall.
- DPO Pilot V1: 66.5%, 85%, 48%.
- DPO Semantic V2: 65.5%, 85%, 46%.

Preference tuning increased KEEP behavior but did not improve detection of
plausible wrong answers. Decision-Only V1 remains the historical router
baseline. Phase 3 protected data must not be reused for tuning.

### Phase 4 — selective correction training

Closed 2026-09-15.

Warm-start V2, GRPO, expanded correction SFT V3, DPO, blind preferences, and
three-round review did not establish reliable autonomous gain. Mean accuracy
fell from 51.25% after round 1 to 49.17% after round 3 in the repeated-review
diagnostic. V2 and V3 are retained as research checkpoints, not promoted
models.

### Phase 5 — correctness signal before feedback

Closed diagnostic.

- Frozen pre-hint probe balanced accuracy: 71.25% for original and V2,
  74.38% for V3.
- Neutral V2 review: 0 fixes, 2 harms.
- Neutral V3 review: 4 fixes, 32 harms.
- Explicit status feedback produced 5 V2 fixes and 7 V3 fixes without observed
  harms, but this is assisted repair.

Conclusion: correctness information is linearly decodable, but the generative
policy does not use it safely. The exact frozen Phase 5 probes were later
recovered and used in Phase 8 v2.

### Phase 6 — signal readout and harness

Closed 2026-09-24.

Verbal confidence correlated with probe ranks but was badly calibrated.
Fuzzy hints and explicit review did not create a safe policy. Signal strength
declined modestly from layer 14 to layer 28 but remained above chance.

On a fresh 40-source holdout, the probe recalled 65–75% of errors, but final
accuracy stayed at 50%. Even an oracle identifying all 20 wrong answers
produced only 1/20, 2/20, and 2/20 repairs for original, V2, and V3.
Repair remained the bottleneck.

### Phase 7 — blind versus answer-visible re-solving

Completed 2026-09-25.

On 80 initially wrong protected sources per checkpoint:

| Checkpoint | Blind fixes | Visible fixes | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Original | 25/80 | 4/80 | +26.25 points |
| V2 | 28/80 | 5/80 | +28.75 points |
| V3 | 32/80 | 8/80 | +30.00 points |

All Holm-adjusted p-values were below 0.00001. Blind re-solving also caused
fewer harms than answer-visible re-solving. This established a strong
answer-visibility effect under the fixed prompts, not a unique psychological
anchoring mechanism.

### Phase 8 — resampling, distractors, and probe routing

Completed 2026-09-30 under execution-lock amendment v2.

The original RTX 3090 v1 host disappeared. The local v1 mirror later also
became unavailable, so the entire experiment was restarted before inspecting
v1 outcomes on one Tesla V100-SXM2-32GB using FP16, vLLM 0.7.0, xFormers, and
ordered batches of 64. The exact frozen Phase 5 probes were recovered and used;
they were not refit. All v2 stages completed and protected results were opened
once.

Fresh pool: 400 GSM8K questions disjoint from Phases 1–7.

First-attempt controls:

| Pass | Accuracy |
|---|---:|
| First answer, temperature 0.7 | 70.75% |
| Second temperature-0.7 sample | 77.25% |
| Greedy, same prompt | 76.75% |

A second answer-hidden attempt alone added about six points.

Three-arm result among 117 initially wrong answers:

| Checkpoint | Blind fixes | Own-visible fixes | Distractor fixes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Original | 50 | 21 | 35/114 |
| V2 | 53 | 17 | 28/114 |
| V3 | 58 | 19 | 29/114 |

Any visible candidate was harmful. An irrelevant wrong answer explained most
of the drop. The additional 9–12 point penalty from showing the model its own
answer did not survive Holm correction, so specific self-anchoring remained
inconclusive.

Probe-routed blind re-solving:

| Checkpoint | KEEP-all | Probe-routed | BLIND-all |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Original | 70.75% | 74.50% | 75.00% |
| V2 | 70.75% | 75.00% | 75.50% |
| V3 | 70.75% | 77.50% | 78.00% |

The preregistered routed gate passed for all three checkpoints. However,
BLIND-all was at least as accurate, and greedy same-prompt performance matched
the routed system closely. The probe mainly saved compute rather than adding
accuracy. No checkpoint was promoted.

Canonical report: `phase8/docs/FINAL_REPORT.md`.

### Phase 9 — independent attempts, voting, and self-check

Closed 2026-10-01.

On 400 protected problems, vote@5 improved 75.0% KEEP accuracy to 84.5%,
85.5%, and 83.25% for original, V2, and V3. The preregistered voting gate
passed for all checkpoints.

Self-check achieved 72.25%, 72.75%, and 74.0% and failed its gate. On pairs
where exactly one answer was correct, the model selected the right answer only
44–52%, near chance. Disagreement itself detected 82–85% of first-answer
errors, but the model could not reliably identify which answer was right.

### Phase 10 — SFT of the judgment step

Closed 2026-10-01, negative result.

A LoRA trained on 723 of the model's own verifier-correct judgments did not
improve selection:

- Right-answer choice: 52.1% untrained to 50.7% trained.
- End-to-end self-check: 73.0% for both.
- vote@5: 82.67%, nine points above KEEP.

Self-imitation taught no useful judgment improvement. The adapter is retained
only for reproducibility.

### Phase 11 — DPO of the judgment step

Closed 2026-10-02, negative primary result.

DPO learned a likelihood preference on unseen validation pairs (67.1%), but
fresh-problem greedy choice changed only from 46.6% to 49.4%, not significant.
The self-check remained worse than agreement-gated voting. vote@5 improved
71.37% to 84.19% (+12.82 points).

Post-hoc analysis separated three effects: position bias, a weak real
correctness sensitivity, and invented third answers.

### Phase 12 — both-orders judging and order-swapped DPO

Closed 2026-10-09. Both preregistered primary endpoints passed on SVAMP.

- Untrained consistent both-orders judgments: 68.7% correct.
- DPO consistent judgments: 74.9% correct at 59.7% coverage.
- DPO self-check was non-inferior to compute-matched vote@3.
- Invented answers remained 10.5%, above the 5% target.
- vote@5 remained best at 90.10%.

This was the first preregistered model-level evidence of a real, trainable
choice signal, although the easier dataset contributed and practical accuracy
still favored voting.

### Phase 13 — transfer, constrained verdicts, and combination

Closed 2026-10-09.

On 561 harder GSM8K test problems:

- The unchanged Phase 12 judge reached 79.3% consistent both-orders accuracy
  at 52.1% coverage; the untrained judge reached 71.0%.
- A constrained-verdict DPO judge reduced invented answers from 9.9% to 2.1%,
  increased coverage to 62.8%, and retained 74.0% consistent accuracy.
- Calling a judge only on split votes failed to beat vote@3 and trailed vote@5
  by 6.6 points.
- vote@5 remained best at 77.54%.

Exploratory probes found the correct-choice signal in layers 16–23 at about
70% cross-dataset accuracy. DPO barely changed this signal; it changed how the
model expressed the decision.

### Phase 14 — generalization and paper

Closed 2026-10-14; paper analyses completed 2026-10-16.

Models:

- Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct
- Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3
- Kxck/Self_Correction_v1
- Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct

Datasets:

- ASDiv
- MATH levels 1–3
- GSM8K
- SVAMP

Preregistered endpoint cells were Llama and Mistral on ASDiv and MATH:

| Cell | Skill index, 95% CI | Order-averaging gain |
|---|---:|---:|
| Llama · ASDiv | +0.321 [0.230, 0.413] | +0.6 points |
| Llama · MATH | +0.362 [0.291, 0.428] | +5.0 [+0.9, +9.3] |
| Mistral · ASDiv | +0.335 [0.269, 0.400] | +0.7 |
| Mistral · MATH | +0.344 [0.261, 0.431] | +3.3 |

E1 passed 4/4: real skill generalized. E2 passed 1/4: log-prob averaging over
both orders was only conditionally useful.

Across the descriptive 4×4 grid:

- Skill index was above zero in 16/16 cells.
- Single-order accuracy was 45–64% under the standard parser.
- Only 51–90% of base judgments made a valid A/B choice.
- Forced-verdict prompts raised valid choice to 79–99%.
- Position bias cost only 0–2.5 accuracy points; format errors, truncation,
  re-solving, and third answers explained much more.
- A rescued parser raised Qwen single-order accuracy from 45–64% to 72–88%,
  showing strong answer-format sensitivity.
- Hidden-state probe AUC ranged about 0.66–0.90.
- Judge strategies lost to vote@3 in 15/16 cells; vote@5 was best throughout.
- Probe-weighted vote@3 generalized out of sample for Llama and Mistral, not
  Self_Correction or Qwen.

Phase 14 produced the published paper. Treat its preregistered, replicated,
descriptive, and exploratory claims as separate evidence tiers. Do not promote
post-hoc analyses to confirmatory findings.

## 7. Current active direction: Phase 15

Status on 2026-10-22: draft design only. Nothing has been generated.

Question: can a 7B model turn one or two verifier-corrected behavior mistakes
into an in-weights LoRA update that improves behavior on new problems after the
correction is removed from context, at least as well as a harness?

The proposed five arms are:

| Arm | Description |
|---|---|
| A0 | Base model, no update |
| A1 | Harness: correction remains in context |
| A2 | Naive LoRA update directly on corrected examples |
| A3 | Untrained model writes a general self-edit, then LoRA trains on it |
| A4 | RL meta-trained self-edit writer, then the same inner LoRA update |

Proposed endpoints:

- P1: A4 improves new problems of meta-training error types by at least five
  points over A0, with CI above zero.
- P2: A4 without correction context is non-inferior to A1, lower CI above
  minus two points.
- P3: A4 does not damage control behavior, lower CI above minus two points.

The strongest secondary test is transfer to held-out error types.

Seven decisions remain open and must be settled before freezing:

1. Main learner: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct or Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct.
2. Source datasets and the dataset reserved for final evaluation.
3. Final eligible error types and meta-train versus held-out type split.
4. Inner LoRA hyperparameters, chosen from dev only.
5. Number of ReST-EM rounds and self-edit samples per episode.
6. Probe/control items and evaluation episode count, informed by pilot power.
7. GPU and budget.

The estimated main experiment is large: roughly 60–110 GPU-hours after pilot.
The design explicitly compares in-weights learning against a harness and
vote@3/vote@5. Every episode starts from the base model; continual accumulation
and long-term forgetting are not tested.

Do not run Phase 15 main generation yet. First complete the pilot design, source
overlap audit, deterministic error detectors, error-type feasibility checks,
power analysis, GPU budget, and execution lock. A valid no-go result is allowed
if the context harness cannot improve any candidate error type.

## 8. Non-negotiable research rules

1. Use deterministic verification, never an LLM judge as correctness oracle.
2. Canonical wrong answers must be natural model failures.
3. Never use frozen evaluation sources for training, threshold selection,
   prompt selection, or sample selection.
4. Freeze hypotheses, endpoints, parsing, seeds, sources, checkpoints, and stop
   rules before protected generation.
5. Open protected endpoints once. Record every amendment before the affected
   run and state whether any output or label had already been inspected.
6. Preserve raw outputs, locks, manifests, hashes, and final reports.
7. Do not call visible rationales hidden chain-of-thought.
8. Report fixes, harms, correct preservation, invalid outputs, and compute, not
   only total accuracy.
9. Separate preregistered, replicated, descriptive, and post-hoc evidence.
10. Do not call an external probe/router/voting system autonomous model-only
    self-correction.
11. New hypotheses require a new phase and a fresh holdout.
12. Do not train on Phase 8–14 protected pools or paper outputs.

## 9. Operational rules

- Work locally first: prepare data, audit overlap, implement, dry-run, freeze
  locks, and package before renting GPU time.
- Save code and results both locally and on the GPU host. Verify hashes; “copied”
  is not sufficient.
- Keep secrets only in `.env`; never print, document, commit, or archive them.
  The Hugging Face variable is exactly `HF_TOKEN`.
- Never assume an old rented GPU endpoint still exists.
- Confirm process state, timestamps, logs, GPU usage, disk space, output counts,
  and hashes before saying a run is active or complete.
- Do not infer progress from stale local mirrors.
- Use FP16 and the exact pinned lineage unless a preregistered amendment states
  otherwise. Quantization is a different experiment.
- Keep all arms of a paired comparison on compatible hardware/runtime and
  document migrations before continuing.
- Do not overwrite a more complete remote audit with a partial local copy.
- Count completion records, not metadata lines.
- Avoid printing raw protected answers while monitoring.
- Report ETA as an estimate based on measured throughput and remaining stages.

## 11. How to communicate with the user

- Speak English unless the user requests Vietnamese.
- The user prefers direct status updates: what is complete, what remains, what
  is verified, and what is uncertain.
- A request such as “continue” authorizes work within the already agreed scope,
  but does not authorize changing a frozen protocol or spending a new GPU
  budget without resolving the Phase 15 open decisions.
- Prepare and repair locally before uploading to reduce GPU cost.
- Use stronger reasoning for experiment design, evaluator code, and scientific
  interpretation. Use lighter monitoring for logs and simple status checks
  when available.
- Do not claim that the Phase 0 goal has been fully solved. The project found
  real internal judgment skill and strong harnesses, but not a standalone model
  that reliably detects and repairs its own errors.
- Do not describe negative phases as useless. They identified the boundaries:
  detection can exist without repair; likelihood shifts can exist without
  decision changes; visible candidate answers can harm; voting is a strong
  baseline; invalid choices can hide real judgment skill.
